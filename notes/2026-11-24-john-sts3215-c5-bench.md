# STS3215 bus servos and LED ring on a XIAO ESP32-C5, no adapter (2026-11-24)

Written by John's AI agent on 2026-11-24, from a bench session with John. Not authoritative. The code is in
[tether_controls PR #4](https://github.com/TerralabsCove/tether_controls/pull/4), under
`bench/boards/xiao-esp32-c5/sts3215_led_test/` (merged). Touches [open question 20](../truth/open-questions.md).

## Results

- **The STS3215 talks to the C5 straight from one GPIO. No adapter board is needed.** The servo data wire goes to
  D2 (GPIO25). The pad is push-pull while sending and open drain while listening. **[verified]** (serial logs below)
- **Open drain alone did not work.** At every baud rate (38400 to 1 Mbit/s) and every ID (0 to 253), and even with a
  broadcast ping, the servo stayed silent. Our own bytes echoed back clean. Switching to push-pull while sending fixed it
  at once. Likely cause: a weak pull-up in the servo gives slow rising edges at 1 Mbit/s. **[assumed]**, no scope.
- **Logic level: 3.3 V and 5 V both work.** The data line idled at 3.07 V against the servo's own GND (multimeter,
  servo unplugged from the C5). C5 GND to servo GND read 0.6 mV. **[verified]** The 12 V spec sheet gives high 2-5 V,
  low 0-0.45 V. See [Spec sheet](#spec-sheet-st-3215-c018).
- **Servo IDs:** one servo is ID 1, the other is ID 2. Someone set ID 2 before. Both answer at 1 Mbit/s and can share
  D2. **[verified]**
- **LED ring:** the WS2812B ring lit on D1 (GPIO0). Red, green, blue and a chase all showed. D0 (GPIO1) did nothing.
  **[verified]** (seen by John)
- **No setup was needed.** The servos answered at factory-style settings: position mode, limits 0-4095.

## Spec sheet (ST-3215-C018)

John found FEETECH's spec sheet for the 12 V, 30 kg·cm model, ST-3215-C018, edition A/0, dated 2023-07-20. The PDF
is not in this repo. These are the sheet's numbers, not measurements. Whether BOM line TTH-090 is exactly this
variant is not confirmed. Check the servo label or the BOM link.

| Item | Sheet value |
|---|---|
| Signal high / low | 2-5 V / 0-0.45 V |
| Bus | Half duplex serial, 8N1. 38400 bit/s to 1 Mbit/s, default 1 Mbit/s. IDs 0-253. |
| Voltage | 12 V rated, 4-14 V range |
| Current | Idle 30 mA, no load 180 mA, rated 900 mA at 10 kg·cm, stall 2.7 A at 30 kg·cm |
| Speed | 0.222 s per 60° (45 rpm) no load |
| Position | 4096 steps per turn, 0.088° per step, centre 2047. Writing 128 to register 40 makes the current position the centre. |
| Modes | 0 position (default), 1 closed-loop speed, 2 open-loop speed, 3 step. Multi-turn ±7 turns, count lost at power off. |
| Connector | 5264-3P: pin 1 GND, pin 2 V+, pin 3 signal |

The servo protects itself. It cuts output when the load stays over 80 % of stall for 2 s, or the current over 2 A
for 2 s. The next position command clears both. It also cuts out above 14 V or below 4 V, until the voltage is back
in range, and cuts torque above 70 °C. The bench firmware's guard trips much sooner.

What this means for us:

- The C5's 3.3 V is in spec. A 5 V board such as an Arduino Nano is in spec too, at the top of the range.
- FEETECH's reference circuit pulls DATA up with 10 kΩ to the MCU's supply and drives it through 74LVC1G126 /
  74LVC1G125 buffers. That fits the finding that open drain with only the C5's internal pull-up got no reply. The
  cause is still **[assumed]**.
- Two servos stalling together could draw over 5 A from the pack.

## Moves

All moves at about 13°/s. Torque was off before and after each test.

| Servo | Test | Result |
|---|---|---|
| ID 1 | ±5°, ±30° | Reached every target within 0.7°. Load stayed low. |
| ID 2 | ±10° (twice), ±20°, torque cap 30 % | Reached every target within 0.7°. Peak load 80-108 of 1000. |
| ID 2 | ±30°, torque cap 30 % | **Blocked on the + side** at position 1731 (about 152°), 1.4° short, load 136. The guard backed it off. John saw the servo mount bend. The − side was free. |
| ID 2 | −50°, torque cap 30 % | Free. Reached position 841 (about 74°) and back. Peak load 104-108. |

Servo ID 2 has at least 50° free on the − side from about 123°. On the + side it touches something between +20° and
+29° from there. Keep + moves under about +20° until that is checked. The servo's own angle limits are still 0-4095.

A 30 % cap still gives about 9 kg·cm on a 30 kg·cm servo. That was enough to bend the mount.

## A C5 died: never run it on the buck and USB together

The first C5 ran on the 5 V buck (fed from the 3S pack) while it was also plugged into the laptop. It got hot. After
that it heated up on USB alone with nothing attached, and the laptop no longer saw it. 5V-to-GND and 3V3-to-GND
still read about 100 kΩ, so the rails are not shorted, but the board is dead. **[verified]**

The harness checked out afterwards: D1 wire about 0 V, D2 wire about 3 V, with the C5 unplugged. So the wiring did not
cause it.

The [USB-host power note](2026-11-20-john-recamera-usb-host-c5.md) already says not to plug the laptop in while the
buck is on. To flash or test from the laptop:

1. Disconnect the buck's + lead at the C5's 5V pad. Keep every ground connected.
2. Plug in USB.
3. When done, unplug USB first, then reconnect the buck's +.

For the robot, a Schottky diode in the buck + to 5V pad line (cathode at the pad) lets USB and the buck both stay
connected. Not built or tested.

An ESP32-S3 used first also stopped responding after one flash. Its cause is unknown.
